ACME City Permits Architecture

This is a local permit application for the fictional ACME City. A master data management (MDM) registry keeps one identity for each person, property and business, and the AI model can only suggest.

Purpose and outcome

This page describes the built local solution. The integrated evidence gate passed on October 27, 2026, so the architecture status is AS BUILT.

One local application holds fictional ACME City records. It stores submissions, decisions, fee versions and evidence in one SQLite database.

The synthetic case SYN-APP-001 is a commercial tenant improvement. It follows one case from a submission that cannot be changed to a simulated permit, issued as a separate step.

Refreshing the page, restarting the server or repeating the issuance returns the same record each time, and the build checks for it.

Two parity phases are also merged. Phase 1 adds an intake agent and an adjudication agent, a confirm and revert flow for the reviewer, 17 permit types and an Administration area. Phase 2 adds a simulated geographic information system (GIS) service, a shared property master, a town map and an applicant portal. Phase 3 and an explainer video are planned only, in their own sections below.

Every record on this page is invented for the demonstration. No permit, payment, parcel or applicant record comes from a real city or a real person.

Determined by code

  • Fee arithmetic from an approved versioned catalog
  • Expected version guards on every change
  • Idempotency on repeated commands
  • Schema and citation checks on every proposal

Decided by a person

  • Steward match and correction decisions
  • Permit review approval
  • Finance allocation of a settled payment
  • Permit issuance, as a separate action

Architecture

One Python service binds loopback only. The browser holds no model credential, and the only outbound call is the bounded model request.

Every component is built. The original build is verified locally, and the final acceptance manifest records the run results. The parity components are covered by the phase 2 merge review under verification. The diagram also shows the parts merged in parity phase 3, listed in the table below. Their evidence is the build log, and the page records no new verification gate for them.

Agents, property master and portal

Agents are woken by a person and run by a person. Only the City Administrator wakes them, and a person with the matching grant runs them. An agent proposes and recommends, and the reviewer decides.

Intake agent, seven nodes

  • Classifier.
  • Field extractor and document quality check, in parallel.
  • Address locator, which proposes a parcel link and never approves it.
  • Duplicate finder and completeness check, in parallel.
  • Router.

Adjudication agent, five nodes

  • Rule loader.
  • Rule evaluator.
  • Condition builder and precedent check, in parallel.
  • Disposition.
  • A held recommendation cannot be confirmed.

A permit decision is blocked until the parcel link is approved and confirmed. The simulated GIS service holds 69 parcels and 71 addresses. Six simulated subscriber departments read the property master: tax and assessor, fire and life safety, code enforcement, utilities, sanitation and police. Their access is set in config/property_subscribers.json.

The applicant portal at /apply and the town map at /map are served by the same local application. The map is drawn on the server, so the browser loads no outside tiles.

Primary process, SYN-APP-001

Eight steps from an applicant submission to a separate simulated permit issuance.

  1. Submit. The applicant submits immutable plans and documents for a fictional tenant improvement. Verified
  2. Receive. The service stores the submission, hashes the payload and opens the intake work. Verified
  3. Ground. The application builds a bounded context from versioned fictional documents and source snapshots. Verified
  4. Propose. A live Standard model call returns a structured extraction proposal, and the application validates it. Verified
  5. Steward. A Data Steward records the match or the correction against exact evidence. Verified
  6. Review. A Permit Reviewer makes an actual review decision on the recorded evaluation. Verified
  7. Assess and settle. The approved catalog prices the case, and Finance confirms the allocation of a settled simulated payment. Verified
  8. Issue. A separate issuance action records the permit, and an identical retry returns the same artifact. Verified

The complete process ran end to end against the local application, with a live model extraction. The steps above show the order and the checks at each step.

Where the parity phases fit

  1. Apply. An applicant chooses one of 17 types at /apply, completes the form, uploads documents, certifies and receives a confirmation. Built
  2. Intake run. A person runs the intake agent. The address locator proposes a parcel link, and the run is recorded with a per-node trace. Built
  3. Link. A person approves and confirms the parcel link. A permit decision stays blocked until then. Built
  4. Adjudication run. A person runs the adjudication agent, which recommends a disposition. Discretionary types are always held for a person. Built
  5. Confirm or revert. The reviewer confirms and is the decider of record, or reverts with a reason and returns the case to manual review. Built
  6. Share. Six simulated departments read the property master and write nothing back. Built

Each kind of data keeps its own owner. The registry records identities and where each record came from. The owning system keeps the facts.

Since phase 2 the GIS source is a simulated service inside the application, with 69 parcels and 71 addresses and no outside calls. The six simulated subscriber departments read property master fields and write nothing back.

Correction routes

Source fact

  • The steward proposes the correction against the exact source version.
  • The application sends a command through the outbox to the owning adapter.
  • The source adapter accepts a new version and acknowledges it.
  • The registry records the new version and its lineage.

Registry identity

  • The steward links or unlinks candidates inside the permitted scope.
  • The decision commits a new crosswalk version.
  • The old version stays reversible and readable.
  • No source fact is edited by the link.

A factual correction becomes official only after the owning system accepts the new version.

Muse contribution and boundary

The model reads a limited set of records and returns a suggestion. It cannot change a source fact, a fee or a permit.

The model may propose

  • Structured application field extraction from versioned fictional documents.
  • Evidence based identity proposals between candidate records.
  • Exact evidence lines that name the source, the version and the location.

The model may never

  • Publish a link or edit a source fact.
  • Calculate authoritative money.
  • Make a reviewer decision or allocate a payment.
  • Issue a permit or write to the database.

Evidence supplied to Muse

The request carries versioned fictional documents, source snapshots, candidate identifiers, allowed visible records, source, effective and receipt times, rule versions and exact evidence lines.

The expected answers used for testing are kept out of every request, so the model never sees them.

Routing and topics

Model muse-spark-1.3 in Standard mode does at least one real live task in the main process, preferably field extraction or an identity proposal.

Requests use a strict JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) Schema, allowlisted identifiers, bounded inputs and outputs, no web search, no tool calls, and concurrency one.

Validation

The application validates identifiers, evidence locations, excerpts, categories and citations before it creates a proposal. A proposal that fails a check is rejected and recorded as rejected.

Evidence

Each run records the mode, the model and response identifiers, prompt and schema versions, input references, output, validation result, available usage, latency and any error.

Live, replay, cached and failed runs stay visibly distinct. Replay is an explicit recovery path and never counts as successful live evidence.

The provider credit balance, expiry, eligible products and the no cash path remain unverified. Live calls stop on provider or billing errors after two successes.

Deterministic controls, fees and idempotency

Money and state change only through versioned, deterministic services and an authorized person.

Controls

  • Stable internal identifiers stay distinct from source identifiers and display numbers.
  • Expected versions protect corrections, links, rules, decisions, allocations and issuance.
  • Submitted revisions, evaluations, decisions, assessments, allocations and issued snapshots stay immutable and versioned.

Idempotency

  • An identical issuance retry returns the same permit record and the same trace.
  • A repeated command with a stale expected version is refused and recorded.
  • A refresh and a restart return the same trace and artifact for the case.

Configuration and discretionary types

A configuration change applies only to new intake. The requirements in force when a case arrived are recorded on the case. Discretionary permit types carry no rule and are always held for a person.

Narration script

Approved Revision 9, rewritten in plain spoken language and approved by the project owner on 2026-10-28. Captions show the written form, such as §14-305 and 6 ft.

Scene 1: the problem

ACME City has one small permit office for the whole town. A homeowner is waiting on a fence permit. A new café can't open yet. And the city council can't see where permits get stuck.

Scene 2: three groups, one app

The app is built for three groups: residents who apply, city staff who review, and city leaders.

Scene 3: residents

Residents can ask the permit assistant anything. Say you want a six foot fence in your front yard. Muse Spark points you to section fourteen three oh five, explains that the limit there is four feet, and tells you what to do next. So you apply already knowing the rule.

Scene 4: city staff

For city staff, agents take a first pass. The intake agent sorts each application, Muse Spark fills in the details from the documents, and a data steward links the applicant, property, and contractor to their master records. The adjudication agent checks the zoning rules and recommends a decision and cites the rule. The permit reviewer makes the final decision.

Scene 5: city leaders

For city leaders, the permit map shows where new restaurants and bars are opening and where the city keeps granting exceptions. The what-if agent tries raising the fence limit from six feet to seven on a year of sample cases. Muse Spark writes the council briefing as a draft for approval.

Scene 6: one shared record

Every resident, property, contractor and permit has one shared record. Fire, police, sanitation and code enforcement all work from it. And on the city's website, residents can look up any permit in their neighbourhood.

Scene 7: close
